Tempo

The time and the weather on your wallpaper, with the forecast a tap away.

ItalianoEnglish

In Italian, tempo means two things at once: what time it is and what the weather is doing. Put the widget on your home screen and you see both, over your own photo, with no frame and no card around them: the figures, the date, the sky right now. Open it and the forecast is all there — hour by hour, day by day, in the places you care about.

Two widgets, no background

  • Large — the time in tall figures, the date underneath, and next to it the weather right now: icon, temperature, today's low.
  • Strip — five cells wide, one line: clock, date, the weather now and the days ahead, three to six of them, however many you ask for.

Neither has a background. Underneath is your photo, exactly as it was.

A colour that follows the sun

The accent is not a shade picked from a list: it is where you are right now between the real sunrise and the real sunset of the place you are in. Blue before dawn, amber as the sun comes up, pale at noon, warm again towards evening. Seven in the morning in December is not the same colour as seven in the morning in June, because it is not the same time of day.

Made to be yours

  • The text — typeface, size and shadow: a bright photo and a dark one do not read the same way.
  • The units — 12 or 24 hour clock, Celsius or Fahrenheit.
  • The strip — how many forecast days fit inside it.
  • The tap — it opens whichever app you want. But a tap on the digits always opens the clock, because when you look at the time it is the alarm you are after.
  • In English and Italian, following the language of the phone.

And a weather app too

The widget says the essential, the app says the rest. Now: temperature, apparent, wind, humidity. Hour by hour, with temperature and rain on two charts sharing the same twenty-four hours. Day by day, with the week's highs and lows. And a day in detail: when it rains and how much, the wind, the UV index with sunrise and sunset. Your places — home, work, where your family lives — are one tap apart.

Your data stays yours

No account, no sign-up, no welcome tour. The app does not ask for your location unless you ask it to: places are searched by name, and "this device" is one choice among them. When you do use it, it is the approximate location and nothing finer — a forecast has no need to know which street you are on — and the coordinates go to Open-Meteo, the only service we contact. Where you have been is not kept: what stays on the phone is the last forecast, so it can be shown without a connection.

How it is paid for

With one anchored banner at the bottom, and nothing else: no ads inside the app, no subscription, no feature held hostage.
