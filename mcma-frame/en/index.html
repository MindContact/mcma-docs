Frame

Your photos gathered by time: bursts draw together, empty days open up.

ItalianoEnglish

It opens on the roll, and the first thing you notice is the space. Ten shots of the same sunset sit overlapped like a dealt hand, because they are almost the same photograph; the ones taken slowly get room of their own; and between Friday's dinner and Sunday's walk there is a gap as tall as the time that actually passed. Scrolling past a week costs a week of thumb.

A tap opens the photograph full screen, and from there you page back and forth across the whole roll, in the order of time. No folders to keep up, no albums to fill: the camera has already written the dates.

What it does

  • It gathers occasions. Shots close together in time become one group, with the hour and how many there are. Nobody had to decide anything: the rule is the distance from one photograph to the next.
  • It draws the pauses. Two hours are a thin rule, three weeks are a space you cross. The time that went by is the thing you see, not a label to read.
  • Colour follows the same number. Amber when the shots chase each other, slate blue when a season went by.
  • Paging, not searching. The viewer runs across the whole roll, with no edges between one afternoon and the next, and shares a photograph through the system sheet.
  • It holds long rolls. Photographs load in blocks as you scroll, and coming back to the app the one you just took is already at the top.
  • It takes a partial yes. If Android lets you pick which photographs to show, Frame works on those and leaves you a way to add more: it is not an error to fix, it is an answer.
  • In English and Italian, following the phone.

A gallery that organises nothing

Other galleries ask for work: albums to create, favourites to mark, faces to confirm. Frame asks for nothing, because the only thing it uses is already inside every photograph — the instant it was taken. Out of that single number come the size of the thumbnails, how much they overlap, the height of the empty space and the colour of the rule: there are no categories to keep tidy, there is a curve. Which is why the app has nothing to learn about you.

Your data stays yours

Frame reads the photographs on your phone and shows them. It does not upload them anywhere, does not copy them into a service, does not send them to us: there is no Frame server and no account. Thumbnails live in the app's memory while the app is open. The only time a photograph leaves the phone is when you share it, and then it goes to the app you picked. The permission for the location recorded inside photographs is never even asked for. What leaves the device is only what Google AdMob collects for the banner, set out in the privacy policy.

How it pays for itself

With a banner anchored at the bottom, and nothing else: no ads inside the app, no subscription, no feature held hostage.
