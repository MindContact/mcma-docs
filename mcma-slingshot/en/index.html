Slingshot

Pull the sling and use the planets’ gravity to reach the target.

ItalianoEnglish

Pull the probe back, let it go, and the gravity of the planets does the rest of the work. While you aim, the predicted arc grows under your thumb — and it is the same maths as the real flight, not a shortcut that resembles it.

What it does

  • Twelve hand-drawn fields, in the order they teach: no gravity at all, then a planet to go around, then a planet to use.
  • The dotted line is the flight, worked out with the same integrator: what you see while aiming is what happens when you let go.
  • The field warms as the probe closes in, and stays lit with how close it got. Even a bad shot tells you how much was missing.
  • A field opens once you have solved the one before it. How many shots it took you stays on the phone.
  • In English and Italian, following the phone's language.

Every field is checked before it ships

A level that only works to the millimetre is not a level, it is a search. Before a field enters the game it is tried with every possible shot — and one solution existing is not enough: there has to be at least two and a half degrees of aim and four per cent of pull of room around it. Five fields were redrawn because they did not have it.

Your data stays yours

Slingshot asks for no permissions beyond the ad network and the vibrator: no sensors, no location, no camera. There is no account and no online leaderboard — that would be a server, and we do not have one. Solved fields stay on the phone.

How it is paid for

One banner anchored at the bottom, and that is all: no ads inside the app, no subscription, no feature held hostage.
