Agenda

Your next events on the home screen, and a calendar that gets out of the way.

ItalianoEnglish

Your appointments are already on the phone. Agenda puts them where you can see them without opening anything — on the home screen, over your wallpaper — and gives you a calendar for when you need to look properly. A tap on an event opens it, a tap on the "+" creates one: none of that goes through a menu.

The widget

Transparent, no frame: your photo shows through. It lists what is coming, each entry with its time and the colour of the calendar it belongs to. The one about to happen carries a bar that shrinks as the moment approaches: the eleven o'clock meeting does not read like Thursday's, because it is not the same thing.

Three ways to look at the same month

  • Month — the whole grid, a dot on the days that have something, and below it the list for the day you tapped.
  • Week — hours down the side, events in their place. Overlapping ones share the width instead of covering each other, and the week opens at the hour you are in.
  • Agenda — only what is ahead, one line after another, no grid at all.

Creating and editing

  • The event — title, place, notes, all day or eleven to twelve, and which calendar it goes to among the ones you can write to.
  • Repeats — daily, weekly, monthly, yearly. And when you edit or delete a repeating event, Agenda asks whether you mean this one time or the whole series: they are two different things and neither is the obvious one.
  • The calendars you want to see — if you have a work calendar, a home one, birthdays and the public holidays of three countries, you do not want them all at once. Settings let you pick which ones show, in the app and in the widget.
  • In English and Italian, following the language of the phone.

These are your calendars, not a copy

What you write here is on the laptop a minute later, because these are rows in the system calendar — the same ones the phone syncs by itself with the account you configured. Agenda asks for no sign-in and creates no profile: it reads what is there and writes back what you change. It does not send the notifications either: your reminders stay the ones you already set.

Your data stays yours

Your events do not leave the device because of us: we have no server, so there is nowhere for them to go. The Internet permission is there for the ads and for nothing else — your calendar does not travel over it. The write permission is asked the first time you tap the "+", not at startup. And turning a calendar off in the settings only hides it: nothing is deleted.

How it is paid for

With one anchored banner at the bottom, and nothing else: no ads inside the app, no subscription, no feature held hostage.
