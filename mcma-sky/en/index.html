Sky

Point the phone: the stars that are up there, right now.

ItalianoEnglish

Point the phone at the sky and look at the screen: what it shows is what is in front of it. Raise your arm and you are at the zenith; lower it and you find the horizon, with north, south, east and west written on it.

2887 stars, inside the app

The stars are neither invented nor downloaded: they are the Bright Star Catalogue, 5th edition (Hoffleit & Warren, 1991) — the one astronomers use — baked into the APK. So the app works up a mountain, on a plane, with the radio off, and it needs to know nothing about you to draw the right sky. The brightest carry their names: Sirius, Vega, Arcturus, Betelgeuse, Rigel, Aldebaran, Antares, Polaris.

How it knows where you are pointing

The magnetometer and the accelerometer, and some geometry: the two sensors give the phone's orientation in space, and from there the projection of whatever is behind the screen. It is the projection a camera makes, so the distances between stars on the glass are the real ones.

The sky then turns on its own, because the sidereal day is four minutes shorter than the solar one — which is why the same stars are overhead four minutes earlier each night, and the app counts it.

In daylight too

By day the screen goes blue and the stars fade, exactly as they do outside — but the app says they are still there: they are, it is the sunlight covering them. And the Sun is where it really is, including when it is below the horizon.

What is not here, and why

The Moon and the planets are not in this version. The short formulae for the Moon's position are a couple of degrees out — five lunar diameters — and a marker placed "about there" is worse than no marker: whoever looks for it in the sky does not find it, and stops trusting the rest as well. They will come from a proper series, or not at all.

Your data stays yours

Sky asks for coarse location and nothing else: not precise location, and no background location. No account, no servers: your position never leaves the phone and we never see it, and the network is needed only for the ads. If you would rather not give one, a list of cities is bundled with the app: you get that city's sky, and it works exactly the same way.

How it is paid for
