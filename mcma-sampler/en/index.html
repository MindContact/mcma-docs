Sampler

Twelve pads with your own sample packs. Everything stays on the phone.

ItalianoEnglish

A grid of twelve pads, and you bring the sounds. It opens with a built-in kit — twelve percussion sounds that are computed, not sampled — so it plays the moment it is installed; then you load your own sample packs and the grid becomes yours.

What it does

  • Twelve pads sounding when the finger goes down, not when it lifts.
  • Every pad can carry a sticker: the picture the pack's author gave it.
  • Switch packs with a tap on the name at the top.
  • Every hit lights the pad and taps the phone under the sound.
  • In English and Italian, following the phone's language.

What a sample pack is

A zip holding a pack.json, twelve audio files and — if you like — twelve images. The json says what the pack is called, who made it, and for each pad the name, the description, the sound file and the sticker. Audio: wav, mp3, ogg, m4a, aac. Images: png, jpg, webp. No tool is needed to make one: it is a folder and a text file.

Careful with the files it opens

Somebody else made that sample pack, and the app treats it accordingly: it refuses archives that are too big, ones that carry paths instead of file names — the trick by which a zip tries to write outside the folder it is opened into — and ones that promise sounds they do not contain. From a pack it does accept, it keeps only the files the pack.json names: everything else stays in the zip.

Your data stays yours

Sampler asks for no storage permission: you pick the file with the system picker, which hands the app that file and nothing else. And there is no network: packs are neither downloaded nor uploaded, they stay on the phone. No account, no server of ours.

How it is paid for

One banner anchored at the bottom, and that is all: no ads between hits, no subscription, no pack held hostage.
