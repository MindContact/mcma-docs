Colmo

Carry the brimful glass to the end of the counter without spilling it.

ItalianoEnglish

The glass is filled to the brim. Tilt the phone to walk, and the more you tilt the faster you go: it is the same tilt that spills it. You do not win or lose — you arrive with whatever is left in it.

What it does

  • Six counters, each with its steps closer together. The difficulty is not the distance: it is how little room there is for the wave to settle between one step and the next.
  • The liquid really sloshes: it comes back, it piles against a wall, and whatever goes over the rim does not come back.
  • The phone warns you when the surface reaches the rim, while there is still time to level off.
  • Putting it down counts too: arriving at a run costs as much as taking a step at a run.
  • Your best on each counter — how full you got there — stays on the phone.
  • In English and Italian, following the phone's language.

A glass sliding freely does not spill

The tilt pours the liquid downhill; the glass accelerating under it throws the liquid the other way. The two nearly cancel, which is why sliding downhill costs nothing at all. What spills a glass is friction, the steps, and a hand that changes its mind — which is exactly what happens to anyone carrying a full glass across a room.

Your data stays yours

Colmo asks for no permissions beyond the ad network, the vibrator and keeping the screen awake while you carry the glass: all it needs is the accelerometer, which asks for none. On a device without one the app says so, instead of showing a glass that never moves. No account, no online leaderboard.

How it is paid for

One banner anchored at the bottom, and that is all: no ads inside the app, no subscription, no feature held hostage.
